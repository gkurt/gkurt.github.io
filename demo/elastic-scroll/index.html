Elastic scroll

Everything below is nailed to the page at its top two corners and tied to your scrollbar with a piece of elastic. Scroll down, the elastic loads up and tears the nails out one at a time; from the second one on, the page starts pulling your scrollbar back. Eventually the elastic frays too, and whatever it was holding falls to the basement — bouncing off everything else on the way down.

↓ Best with a mouse wheel. Open the panel top-right to change the physics.

Five laws

  1. 01 Two nails hold each element in its slot. One long elastic ties it to the scrollbar thumb.
  2. 02 The elastic only pulls while the thumb is below the element. Above it, it just trails along — nothing to fight about yet.
  3. 03 Nails are weaker than the elastic, so they go first: one lets go, the element swings on the other, then that one goes too.
  4. 04 Only an element that has already lost a nail pulls back on your scrollbar. Until then the wall is taking the load, not you.
  5. 05 Everything loose is a matter-js rigid body with mass and rotation. Heavy things are stubborn, light things fly, and they all collide on the way down.
